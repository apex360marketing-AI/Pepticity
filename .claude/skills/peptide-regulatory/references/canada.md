## 2. HEALTH CANADA ENFORCEMENT ON PEPTIDES (2024-2026)
- **"Think twice before injecting peptides bought online"** (about April 2026; CP24 quoted it 2026-04-17). Names BPC-157, CJC-1295, DSIP, Epitalon, GHK-Cu, HCG, Ipamorelin, KPV, Melanotan I and II, MOTS-c, NAD+, SS-31, TB-500, Retatrutide, and says many other unauthorized peptide drugs exist and should all be avoided. "Unauthorized drug products are illegal in Canada." [S1] [VERIFY exact publication date on the page]
- **Canada Peptide seizure** (CP24 2025-08-03): about 50 products including BPC-157, Retatrutide, Tirzepatide and Sermorelin; none authorized [S2].
- **Optimum Wellness Centre, Calgary** seizure of injectable peptides (from S0) [S5].
- **Canlab Research permanent injunction**: a Québec court granted it on 2026-06-11, barring the sale of unauthorized injectable peptides in Canada; Health Canada statement 2026-07-29 [S4]. **This is the closest precedent to a Canadian online research-peptide store.**
- **Galena Pharm** (2021): unauthorized injectables including a reduced glutathione injection with high endotoxin; recall and stop-sale [S53a].
- **"Research use only" labels** do not make a product legal for human use (Health Canada, via S1 and McMaster S3).

## 3. COMPOUNDING AND PROVINCIAL COLLEGES
- **POL-0051** (Policy on Manufacturing and Compounding Drug Products): compounding is for an individual patient in exceptional circumstances; non-patient-specific production is manufacturing; compounding "must not be used to circumvent" drug review [S19].
- **GLP-1s:** Health Canada's June 2025 position, quoted by the Alberta College of Pharmacy: some pharmacies "continue to prepare and sell injectable GLP-1 products in the absence of a shortage, which is not permitted"; there is no shortage; semaglutide-plus-B6 mixtures have no supporting evidence; a recall of two compounded products followed [S20].
- **Ontario College of Pharmacists** guidance (updated 2024-01-05, written during the Ozempic shortage) applies POL-0051: compound only during a shortage, with documented medical need, as a customized solution for one patient [S20]. The earlier research file reports OCP signalling that compounding with unauthorized ingredients could be investigated [S0] [VERIFY current OCP statement].
- **CBC Investigates (2026):** doctors in Ontario and BC prescribing BPC-157 via compounding; prescribers appear to be in private clinics [S19] [VERIFY details in the article].
- **NAPRA model standards** for sterile and non-sterile compounding apply in most provinces [VERIFY current versions]. Not covered by this session's searches.
- **Prescribers:** clinicians must be licensed in the patient's province; colleges (CPSO, CPSBC, CPSA and others) have virtual-care policies [VERIFY each]. Advertising by physicians is also governed by their college rules (no testimonials, no superlatives in most provinces [VERIFY]).

## 4. COMPETITION ACT
- **s.74.01(1)(a)** civil: false or misleading representations in a material respect. **s.52** criminal for knowing or reckless ones. The general impression counts, including images [S15].
- **s.74.01(1)(b)** performance claims need an "adequate and proper test" done **before** the claim (NuvoCare, weight-loss claims) [S15].
- **Drip pricing** (ss. 52(1.3), 74.01(1.1), since 2022): a price that can't be paid because of mandatory extra fees is misleading. Cineplex: C$38.9M (2024). Federal Court, Oct 2026 (Ticketmaster): focus on concealment and sequence [S16, S17].
- **Bill C-59:** private parties can bring deceptive-marketing cases to the Competition Tribunal from **2025-06-20** (restitution available) [S18]. AMPs for deceptive marketing can reach up to 3% of worldwide gross revenues [S18] [VERIFY formula]. Practical effect: competitors can now sue over a rival's health claims.
- **Testimonials and reviews:** a review or testimonial is the advertiser's representation; fake or incentivized reviews without disclosure are misleading [VERIFY Bureau guidance]. Under S0, invented stats, reviews or star ratings are banned at PEPTICITY anyway.

## 5. CASL (ANTI-SPAM)
- Express consent: **no pre-ticked boxes**, a separate box per consent type [S64].
- Identify the sender, include a working unsubscribe, honour it promptly (within 10 business days [VERIFY]).
- Transactional messages (booking confirmations) differ from marketing (newsletters). Keep them on separate consents.
- "Email me this summary" is a consented request; "send me PEPTICITY news" is a separate, unticked box.

## 6. PRIVACY
- **PIPEDA:** health-related information is sensitive. Implied consent is inappropriate for health-based remarketing; **express consent** is required, and key consent information goes up front, not buried [S63].
- **Ontario PHIPA:** applies to health information custodians (clinicians, pharmacies, labs) and their agents. Consent must be knowledgeable, related to the information, voluntary [S63]. If PEPTICITY's clinicians or pharmacy are custodians, PEPTICITY may be their agent [VERIFY with counsel].
- **Québec Law 25:** described as Canada's strictest private-sector regime: a named privacy officer, privacy impact assessments (including before transfers outside Québec), separate consent for sensitive information, and opt-in for tracking technologies [VERIFY details].
- **Alberta HIA / PIPA, BC PIPA, other provincial health-information acts** apply by province [VERIFY each].
- **Rule for the build:** no quiz or bot health text goes to ad, analytics or session-replay tools. Store only with consent, minimum necessary.

## 9. AI AND CHATBOTS IN CANADA
- No federal AI statute in force (the proposed AIDA did not pass [VERIFY]). No formal CMA, CMPA or Health Canada guidance on consumer health chatbots was found [S0].
- Existing law still applies: Competition Act (misleading impressions, including implying a human or clinician), privacy law, and professional-college rules on who may give medical advice.
- Public sentiment (S0, S67): Canadians who followed AI health advice were five times more likely to report harm (CMA/Abacus survey); 68% would rather wait two weeks for a human doctor. "A real clinician decides" is both the law-safe and the trust-building line.
- Crisis line: **9-8-8** (call or text, 24/7, English and French) [S66]; emergencies **911**.
