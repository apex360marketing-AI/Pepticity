# PEPTIDE GLOSSARY

Plain-English definitions for staff, copy and the PEPTICITY GUIDE. **Define, never instruct.** If a definition would naturally lead to "so you should…", stop at the definition and add: "Your clinician and pharmacy handle that."

## Chemistry and biology
- **Amino acid**: one of about 20 building blocks the body uses to make peptides and proteins.
- **Peptide**: a short chain of amino acids (roughly 2 to 50). Dipeptide = 2, tripeptide = 3 (GHK-Cu, KPV, glutathione), tetrapeptide = 4 (Epitalon, SS-31), pentadecapeptide = 15 (BPC-157).
- **Protein**: a long chain of amino acids that folds into a shape. Growth hormone (191 amino acids) is a protein.
- **Receptor**: a "lock" on a cell that a hormone or medicine fits into.
- **Agonist**: something that switches a receptor on. **Antagonist**: something that blocks it.
- **Dual / triple agonist**: switches on two or three different receptors (tirzepatide: two; retatrutide: three).
- **Incretin**: a gut hormone released after eating that helps control blood sugar and fullness (GLP-1, GIP).
- **GLP-1**: glucagon-like peptide-1, an incretin. "A GLP-1" in casual speech means a medicine that acts on its receptor.
- **GIP**: glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide, the second incretin.
- **Glucagon**: a pancreatic hormone that raises blood sugar and affects energy use.
- **Growth hormone (GH, somatropin)**: a pituitary hormone involved in growth and metabolism.
- **GHRH**: growth-hormone-releasing hormone, the brain signal that tells the pituitary to release GH.
- **Ghrelin**: the "hunger hormone"; its receptor also triggers GH release.
- **Secretagogue**: something that makes a gland release (secrete) a hormone.
- **IGF-1**: insulin-like growth factor 1, made mostly by the liver in response to GH.
- **Melanocortins**: a hormone family (including alpha-MSH) acting on receptors involved in skin colour, appetite, inflammation and sexual function.
- **Thymus / thymosins**: a gland behind the breastbone that trains immune cells; thymosins are peptides it makes.
- **Mitochondria**: the parts of cells that make energy.
- **Coenzyme**: a helper molecule an enzyme needs to work (NAD+).
- **Telomeres / telomerase**: protective caps on chromosome ends, and the enzyme that maintains them.
- **Bioregulator**: a term from Russian research for very short peptides claimed to regulate genes in specific tissues. The term has no regulatory meaning in Canada or the US.

## Drug-development and evidence words
- **Analogue (analog)**: a modified copy of a natural molecule, changed to last longer or bind more strongly.
- **Fragment**: a piece cut from a larger natural peptide or protein (TB-500, AOD-9604, KPV).
- **Half-life**: the time it takes the body to clear half of a substance. It explains why some medicines are weekly and others daily. Schedules are a clinician's decision.
- **DAC (Drug Affinity Complex)**: a chemical add-on that makes a peptide stick to a blood protein (albumin) so it lasts much longer (CJC-1295 with DAC).
- **Preclinical**: lab-dish (in vitro) or animal (in vivo) research, before testing in people.
- **Phase 1 / 2 / 3**: human trial stages: first safety, then early effect and dose-finding, then large confirmatory trials.
- **RCT (randomized controlled trial)**: people are randomly assigned to the product or a comparison (often placebo). The strongest single design.
- **Placebo**: an inactive look-alike used for comparison.
- **Primary endpoint**: the main result a trial was designed to test. Missing it means the trial failed on its main question, even if other results look good.
- **Topline results**: a company's early headline summary, before peer review.
- **Peer review**: independent experts check a study before a journal publishes it.
- **Systematic review / meta-analysis**: a structured summary of all studies on a question / a statistical pooling of them.
- **Case report**: a description of one or a few patients. Can raise a safety signal; cannot prove cause.
- **Off-label**: a licensed clinician prescribing an approved drug for something not on its label. Lawful for clinicians in many cases; never something a seller may advertise.
- **Accelerated approval (US)**: approval based on a measure likely to predict benefit, with a confirmatory trial required (elamipretide).

## Regulatory and supply words
- **Approved / authorized**: a regulator has reviewed safety, effectiveness and quality for a specific product and use. In Canada, a **DIN** (Drug Identification Number) and a **NOC** (Notice of Compliance) show this. In the US, an NDA or BLA approval.
- **Prescription Drug List (Canada) / Rx-only (US)**: medicines that need a prescription.
- **Unauthorized drug (Canada) / unapproved new drug (US)**: sold for human use without that review. Illegal to sell; in Canada also illegal to advertise.
- **Compounding**: a pharmacy preparing a medicine for one patient's specific need. Not approved, not reviewed for effectiveness, and never "the same as" an approved product.
- **503A / 503B (US)**: the two compounding routes. 503A = state-licensed pharmacies making patient-specific prescriptions. 503B = registered "outsourcing facilities" that can make larger batches under stricter manufacturing rules.
- **Bulks list / Category 1 / Category 2 (US)**: FDA lists of bulk ingredients 503A pharmacies may use. Category 2 = "significant safety concerns", so not compoundable while there.
- **PCAC**: FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee. Its votes are recommendations, not decisions.
- **Shortage list**: when a medicine is in official shortage, compounding copies is allowed more widely. When the shortage ends, that permission ends.
- **Research use only (RUO)**: a label for chemicals sold to labs, not people. Regulators in both countries say it does not make human sale legal.
- **COA (certificate of analysis)**: a lab report on one batch (lot): identity, purity, sometimes sterility and endotoxin. A COA is not approval.
- **Lot / batch**: one production run. The lot code links a vial to its COA.
- **Endotoxin**: toxic material from bacteria that can contaminate injectables (Health Canada's 2021 glutathione recall found high levels).
- **Sterile / aseptic**: free of living microbes / prepared in a way that keeps it that way.

## Product-handling words (definitions only)
- **Lyophilized**: freeze-dried into a powder so it keeps longer.
- **Reconstitution**: turning a powder back into a liquid before use. PEPTICITY never gives volumes, ratios or calculators. "Your pharmacy and clinician handle preparation."
- **Diluent**: the liquid used for reconstitution (for example bacteriostatic water).
- **Bacteriostatic**: contains a preservative that stops bacteria growing.
- **Subcutaneous / intramuscular / intranasal / topical**: under the skin / into muscle / in the nose / on the skin. Define if asked; never say which a person should use.
- **Cold chain**: keeping a product refrigerated from pharmacy to door.

## Words PEPTICITY does not use as claims
"Healing", "repair", "recovery", "boost", "burn", "anti-aging", "longevity", "detox", "optimize", "stack", "protocol", "cycle", "pharmaceutical grade", "research grade", "clinically proven". Full list in `peptide-regulatory/references/wording-rules.md`.
