## 1. TIRZEPATIDE
- **Aliases:** tirz, Mounjaro, Zepbound; people loosely call it "a GLP-1".
- **Class:** Dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist (incretin medicine).
- **What it is:** The active ingredient in Eli Lilly's prescription medicines Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (US, chronic weight management, and obstructive sleep apnea with obesity [VERIFY]).
- **Mechanism:** It copies two gut hormones that the body releases after eating. They signal fullness, slow stomach emptying, and help the pancreas release insulin when blood sugar is high.
- **Evidence:** Rung 1. Large phase 3 RCT programmes: SURPASS (type 2 diabetes) and SURMOUNT (weight), for example Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022 (SURMOUNT-1) [VERIFY citation details] [S40]. These trials studied Lilly's approved product, not compounded or research-vial versions.
- **Approved anywhere:** Yes. FDA approved Zepbound for chronic weight management on 2023-11-08 [S12]. Health Canada issued a Notice of Compliance for Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes on 2022-11-24 [S11]. A Canadian weight-management indication was **not** found in this research [VERIFY in the Drug Product Database before saying anything].
- **Canada:** Prescription-only. Health Canada's June 2025 position (quoted by the Alberta pharmacy regulator) says compounding injectable GLP-1 products without a shortage "is not permitted", and that there is no shortage [S20]. Tirzepatide appears in the list of products seized from Canada Peptide (2025 advisory) [S2]. So a compounded or research tirzepatide sold in Canada is, on Health Canada's stated position, unlawful. **Counsel question.**
- **US:** FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved on 2024-12-19. The 503A grace period ended 2025-02-18 and the 503B grace period 2025-03-19 [S21]. FDA proposed in May 2026 to exclude tirzepatide (with semaglutide and liraglutide) from the 503B bulks list [S22]. Warning-letter waves (Sept 2025, March 2026, June 2026) hit telehealth firms claiming compounded versions were the "same active ingredient", "clinically studied" or from an "FDA-approved pharmacy" [S23, S24]. FDA's August 2026 letter to Royal Peptides named "Tirzepatide" as an unapproved new drug when sold as a research product [S25].
- **Clinician screens for (topics):** personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (a contraindication in the monographs); pancreatitis; gallbladder disease; type 1 diabetes; insulin or sulfonylurea use (low blood sugar); severe stomach or bowel conditions; kidney function and dehydration; pregnancy, plans to conceive, breastfeeding; oral contraceptives (US label interaction topic [VERIFY Canadian PM]); diabetic eye disease [VERIFY]; eating-disorder history; upcoming surgery or sedation [VERIFY].
- **Storage basics:** The branded pens are refrigerated, with a limited room-temperature allowance on the label [VERIFY per label]. Compounded or vial products follow the pharmacy label.
- **Say:** "Tirzepatide is the active ingredient in the prescription medicines Mounjaro and Zepbound. A licensed clinician decides if any medicine is right for you."
- **Don't say:** "Same as Mounjaro", "generic Mounjaro", "compounded tirzepatide", "lose up to 20%", "clinically proven", "FDA-approved pharmacy", any trial number beside the name, any goal beside the name.
- **Store-page note:** Long description is placeholder text (soap ingredients and "lorem ipsum").

## 2. RETATRUTIDE
- **Aliases:** reta, "triple G", LY3437943 [VERIFY code].
- **Class:** Triple GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist (investigational).
- **What it is:** An Eli Lilly investigational medicine still in clinical trials.
- **Mechanism:** Like tirzepatide, plus a third signal (glucagon) that acts on energy use and the liver.
- **Evidence:** Rung 3. Phase 2 published (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2023 [VERIFY]). Lilly announced positive phase 3 topline results (TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3) on 2026-07-23. These are company-reported, not yet peer-reviewed. Gastrointestinal side effects dominated, and stopping because of side effects was more common than on placebo. Lilly plans to file with FDA in Q1 2027 [S26].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's "Think twice before injecting peptides bought online" advisory (around April 2026) and in the Canada Peptide seizure list [S1, S2]. Unauthorized: illegal to sell.
- **US:** Not approved, so it cannot be lawfully compounded as an approved-drug copy, and it is not on the 503A bulks list [VERIFY reasoning with counsel]. FDA's August 2026 Royal Peptides letter named retatrutide as an unapproved new drug [S25].
- **Clinician screens for:** The same incretin topics as tirzepatide, plus the fact that long-term safety is still unknown.
- **Storage basics:** Not applicable (no lawful product to dispense).
- **Say:** "Retatrutide is an investigational medicine that is still in clinical trials. It isn't approved in Canada or the US."
- **Don't say:** Anything implying availability, "next-gen Ozempic", trial weight-loss figures, "coming soon".
- **Store-page note:** Placeholder text. **Recommendation: do not list on PEPTICITY.**

## 3. TESAMORELIN
- **Aliases:** Egrifta, Egrifta SV, Egrifta WR, tesa.
- **Class:** GHRH analogue.
- **What it is:** The active ingredient in Theratechnologies' prescription medicine Egrifta.
- **Mechanism:** It copies the brain's growth-hormone-releasing hormone, so the pituitary releases more of the body's own growth hormone.
- **Evidence:** Rung 1, for one indication. Phase 3 RCTs in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat (for example Falutz et al., NEJM 2007 [VERIFY]). FDA approved the new F8 formulation (Egrifta WR) on 2025-03-25, bioequivalent to the original [S27].
- **Approved anywhere:** Yes, narrowly: to reduce excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy. Health Canada issued a Notice of Compliance in April 2014. The Canadian label says it is not indicated for weight-loss management [S28]. Whether it is still marketed in Canada was not confirmed [VERIFY].
- **Canada:** Prescription-only for the labelled use. A research or compounded copy is not the authorized product.
- **US:** FDA's August 2026 letter named "Tesamorelin" sold by a research-peptide seller as an unapproved new drug [S25]. WADA lists tesamorelin among banned GH secretagogues [S30].
- **Clinician screens for:** active cancer or cancer history; pregnancy; pituitary or hypothalamic conditions; glucose intolerance or diabetes; joint pain and fluid retention (common label side effects: joint pain, injection-site reactions, swelling) [S27]; IGF-1 monitoring [VERIFY]; tested athlete.
- **Storage basics:** Per the product label (formulations differ) [VERIFY].
- **Say:** "Tesamorelin is the active ingredient in the prescription medicine Egrifta, which is approved for a specific condition in people living with HIV."
- **Don't say:** "Belly-fat peptide", "GH booster", "anti-aging", any benefit for people outside the label.

## 4. AOD-9604
- **Aliases:** AOD, "Advanced Obesity Drug", hGH fragment 177-191 [VERIFY exact fragment].
- **Class:** Growth-hormone fragment.
- **What it is:** A lab-made, modified piece of the end of the human growth-hormone molecule.
- **Mechanism:** It was designed to keep growth hormone's effect on fat breakdown without its effect on growth or blood sugar. In people, that hope was not confirmed.
- **Evidence:** Rung 4, with a failed pivotal trial. A 24-week phase IIb trial in 536 people with obesity did not beat placebo on weight loss, and Metabolic Pharmaceuticals ended obesity development in March 2007 [S31, secondary sources; VERIFY against ASX filings].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Unauthorized drug. The earlier research file reports AOD-9604 named in Health Canada advisories [S0] [VERIFY which advisory].
- **US:** Status conflicting. FDA's 2024 document says nominations were withdrawn, removing it from Category 2. HHS said on 2026-02-27 that about 14 of 19 Category 2 peptides, including AOD-9604, would move back to Category 1. Later trackers disagree [S32]. Treat as **not lawfully compoundable until counsel confirms**. It was one of four peptides in a Texas lawsuit over Category 2 placement [S33].
- **Clinician screens for:** goal and approved alternatives; cancer history (GH-axis); unknown long-term safety; tested athlete [VERIFY WADA section].
- **Storage basics:** Research vials are freeze-dried; no authorized product exists.
- **Say:** "AOD-9604 is a lab-made fragment of human growth hormone. It isn't approved as a medicine anywhere."
- **Don't say:** "Fat-burning peptide", "FDA GRAS", "clinically tested for weight loss".
- **Store-page note:** Placeholder text.

## 5. 5-AMINO-1MQ
- **Aliases:** 5-amino, 1MQ, 5A1MQ.
- **Class:** Not a peptide. A small-molecule NNMT (nicotinamide N-methyltransferase) inhibitor.
- **What it is:** A lab-made small molecule sold alongside peptides.
- **Mechanism:** It blocks an enzyme found in fat tissue that uses up a building block of NAD+. In mice this changed how fat cells use energy.
- **Evidence:** Rung 5. Mouse studies only: an 11-day proof-of-concept and a 28-day study in diet-induced obese mice [S34]. No published human trials found [S34].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Unauthorized drug if sold for human use. Not seen in the 2026 advisory list [VERIFY].
- **US:** Unapproved new drug if marketed for human use.
- **Clinician screens for:** no human data; liver and kidney function; current medicines.
- **Say:** "5-Amino-1MQ is a small molecule, not a peptide. It has only been studied in animals."
- **Don't say:** "Fat-cell shrinker", "metabolism activator", "clinically shown".

## 7. BPC-157
- **Aliases:** BPC, "Body Protection Compound", PL 14736 [VERIFY].
- **Class:** Synthetic gastric peptide fragment (15 amino acids).
- **What it is:** A lab-made peptide based on a fragment of a protein found in human gastric juice.
- **Mechanism:** In animal studies it affects blood-vessel growth and tissue repair signals. How, or whether, this happens in people is unknown.
- **Evidence:** Rung 4. A 2025 orthopaedic systematic review kept 36 studies, 35 preclinical and one human. Overall, about three small human studies with fewer than 30 people total, none randomized. An IV pilot gave it to two healthy adults with no control group. A 42-person phase I trial registered in 2015 never published results [S38]. McMaster researchers: "hype is outrunning the evidence" [S3].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory and the Canada Peptide seizure [S1, S2]. CBC reported doctors in Ontario and BC prescribing it through compounding; Health Canada says compounding "must not be used to circumvent" review [S0, S19].
- **US:** Removed from Category 2 on 2026-04-15 (effective 2026-04-22). The PCAC voted 8-6 (1 abstention) on 2026-07-23 to recommend it for the 503A bulks list, against FDA staff's recommendation. Not final; FDA has not added it [S36, S37]. Prohibited by WADA at all times (named under S0) [S30].
- **Clinician screens for:** cancer history (theoretical concern because of its effect on blood-vessel growth [VERIFY]); pregnancy; unknown long-term safety; product purity and source; tested athlete.
- **Say:** "BPC-157 is a lab-made peptide based on a fragment of a stomach protein. It isn't approved as a medicine in Canada or the US, and human research is very limited."
- **Don't say:** "Heals", "repairs tendons", "gut healing", "Wolverine", "body protection" as a benefit, "safe with no side effects".

## 8. TB-500
- **Aliases:** thymosin beta-4 fragment, TB4 frag, "LKKTETQ" [S36].
- **Class:** Synthetic thymic peptide fragment.
- **What it is:** A lab-made peptide based on part of thymosin beta-4, a protein the body makes.
- **Mechanism:** Full thymosin beta-4 helps cells move and organize their internal scaffolding (actin) in lab studies. Whether the short TB-500 fragment does the same in people is unstudied.
- **Evidence:** Rung 5 for TB-500 itself (no human trials found [VERIFY]). The full-length protein as eye drops (RGN-259) had mixed results: small positive phase 2s, then a 317-person phase II/III and a 700-person phase III that missed primary endpoints, with some secondary signals [S39]. Eye-drop results say nothing about an injected fragment.
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1].
- **US:** Removed from Category 2 April 2026; PCAC voted 8-6 (1 abstention) to recommend, July 2026; not final [S36, S37]. Prohibited by WADA [S30] [VERIFY section].
- **Clinician screens for:** cancer history [VERIFY rationale]; unknown long-term safety; source purity; tested athlete.
- **Say:** "TB-500 is a lab-made peptide based on part of thymosin beta-4, a protein the body makes. It isn't approved as a medicine."
- **Don't say:** "Recovery peptide", "injury repair", "flexibility".

## 11. KPV
- **Aliases:** Lys-Pro-Val, alpha-MSH (11-13).
- **Class:** Melanocortin-derived tripeptide.
- **What it is:** A three-amino-acid fragment of a hormone called alpha-MSH.
- **Mechanism:** In mouse and cell studies it calmed inflammatory signalling in the gut. It enters gut cells through a transporter called PepT1.
- **Evidence:** Rung 5. Mouse colitis models (Kannengiesser et al., Inflamm Bowel Dis 2008; Dalmasso et al., 2008). No human efficacy trials found [S42].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1].
- **US:** Removed from Category 2 April 2026; PCAC voted 8-6 (1 abstention) to recommend, July 2026; not final [S36, S37].
- **Clinician screens for:** any diagnosed bowel disease (needs proper care first); unknown human safety.
- **Say:** "KPV is a three-amino-acid fragment of a hormone called alpha-MSH. It hasn't been tested in human trials."
- **Don't say:** "Gut healer", "anti-inflammatory", "for IBD".

## 12. THYMOSIN ALPHA-1
- **Aliases:** Ta1, TA-1, thymalfasin, Zadaxin.
- **Class:** Thymic immune peptide (28 amino acids [VERIFY]).
- **What it is:** A copy of a peptide made by the thymus gland. As thymalfasin (Zadaxin) it is approved as a medicine in some countries.
- **Mechanism:** Acts on immune signalling (toll-like receptor pathways) [S43].
- **Evidence:** Rung 2. Approved in roughly 35 countries, mainly for chronic hepatitis B, first in China in 1995 [S43]. Not FDA-approved for any indication [S43].
- **Approved anywhere:** Yes, abroad (not US; Canada not authorized per one secondary source [VERIFY in DPD]).
- **Canada:** Not authorized [VERIFY]. Not in the 2026 advisory list seen.
- **US:** Presented to the PCAC on 2024-12-04; FDA proposed **not** adding it to the 503A bulks list. Outcome and current status not confirmed [VERIFY] [S44]. Part of the Texas Category 2 lawsuit [S33].
- **Clinician screens for:** autoimmune disease; immunosuppressant medicines; organ transplant; pregnancy; active infection that needs proper care.
- **Say:** "Thymosin Alpha-1, also called thymalfasin, is approved as a medicine in some countries. It isn't approved in the US. [Canadian status: confirm]"
- **Don't say:** "Immune booster", "for long COVID", "antiviral".
- **Store-page note:** Page shows the TB-500 description by mistake.

## 13. HGH (HUMAN GROWTH HORMONE)
- **Aliases:** somatropin, GH, "growth".
- **Class:** Protein hormone (191 amino acids: technically a protein, not a short peptide).
- **What it is:** A prescription-only medicine for specific diagnosed conditions (for example growth-hormone deficiency in children and adults) [VERIFY full indication list per brand].
- **Mechanism:** It acts on the body's growth-hormone receptors and makes the liver produce IGF-1.
- **Evidence:** Rung 1 for diagnosed deficiency and labelled conditions only.
- **Approved anywhere:** Yes, as licensed brand products.
- **Canada:** Prescription Drug List medicine [VERIFY]. A research-store vial is not a licensed somatropin product.
- **US:** **Highest legal risk in the catalogue.** Under 21 U.S.C. § 333(e) it is a federal crime to distribute, or possess with intent to distribute, HGH for any use other than an authorized indication under a physician's order. FDA's import alert says no HGH use for anti-aging, bodybuilding or athletic enhancement is authorized. Up to 5 years in prison, 10 if a minor is involved [S45]. [VERIFY current subsection lettering.] Banned by WADA at all times [S30].
- **Clinician screens for:** proper diagnosis with testing; active cancer or cancer history; diabetes and blood sugar; intracranial pressure symptoms; joint pain, swelling, carpal tunnel; sleep apnea [VERIFY label list].
- **Say:** "HGH (somatropin) is a prescription-only medicine for specific diagnosed conditions. A licensed clinician decides if it is ever appropriate."
- **Don't say:** "Anti-aging", "youth hormone", "muscle", "fat loss", any lifestyle use at all.
- **Store-page note:** Title says 100 iu, description says 1000 iu. **Recommendation: do not list on PEPTICITY**; any lawful supply comes from a pharmacy filling a licensed brand for a diagnosed condition.

## 14. CJC-1295 (WITH DAC / WITHOUT DAC)
- **Aliases:** CJC, Mod GRF 1-29 (often used for "without DAC") [VERIFY], DAC = Drug Affinity Complex.
- **Class:** GHRH analogue.
- **What it is:** Lab-made versions of growth-hormone-releasing hormone. "DAC" is an add-on that binds to a blood protein (albumin) so it lasts much longer.
- **Mechanism:** Signals the pituitary to release growth hormone. A 2006 phase 1 study reported raised growth hormone and IGF-1 for days after one dose [S46].
- **Evidence:** Rung 4. Phase 1 in healthy adults (Teichman et al., JCEM 2006 [VERIFY]). A 192-person phase II trial in HIV-related abdominal fat was halted in July 2006 after a participant died; the link to the drug was never established in the sources found [S46]. No phase 3.
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1].
- **US:** Category 2 status conflicting: still Category 2 per FDA-list trackers; HHS announced a planned move back to Category 1 on 2026-02-27 [S32] [VERIFY]. Part of the Texas lawsuit [S33]. WADA bans it (GH secretagogues) [S30].
- **Clinician screens for:** cancer history; blood sugar; pituitary conditions; tested athlete.
- **Say:** "CJC-1295 is a lab-made version of growth-hormone-releasing hormone. It isn't approved as a medicine."
- **Don't say:** "GH stack", "with ipamorelin", "sleep and recovery".

## 15. IPAMORELIN

- **Class:** Growth-hormone secretagogue (ghrelin receptor agonist), five amino acids [VERIFY].
- **What it is:** A lab-made peptide in the growth-hormone secretagogue family.
- **Mechanism:** Acts on the ghrelin ("hunger hormone") receptor, which triggers growth-hormone release.
- **Evidence:** Rung 4. A 117-patient Helsinn phase 2 trial after bowel surgery (NCT00672074) did not reach significance on its primary endpoint (p = 0.15); published Beck et al., Int J Colorectal Dis 2014 [S47]. No approval.
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1].
- **US:** Still Category 2 per most trackers (not among the 12 April 2026 removals); conflicting HHS statement [S32]. Texas lawsuit [S33]. WADA bans it [S30].
- **Clinician screens for:** cancer history; blood sugar; tested athlete.
- **Say:** "Ipamorelin is a lab-made peptide in the growth-hormone secretagogue family. It isn't approved as a medicine."
- **Don't say:** "Gentle GH release", "no side effects", "sleep peptide".

## 16. IGF-1 LR3
- **Aliases:** IGF, IGF-1, LR3, Long R3 IGF-1.
- **Class:** Growth-factor analogue.
- **What it is:** A lab-made, longer-lasting version of insulin-like growth factor 1, originally made for cell-culture labs [VERIFY].
- **Mechanism:** Acts like the body's IGF-1 (the growth factor growth hormone triggers) but binds less to the proteins that normally hold IGF-1 in check, so more of it is active [VERIFY].
- **Evidence:** Rung 5. Never tested in a human clinical trial [S48]. The only approved IGF-1 medicine, mecasermin (Increlex), is a different molecule for severe primary IGF-1 deficiency in children, and its label warns of severe low blood sugar and seizures [S48].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Unauthorized drug. Not in the 2026 list seen [VERIFY].
- **US:** Unapproved new drug. WADA bans IGF-1 and analogues [S30] [VERIFY].
- **Clinician screens for:** low blood sugar risk; diabetes; cancer history; tested athlete.
- **Say:** "IGF-1 LR3 is a lab-made, longer-lasting version of a growth factor called IGF-1. It has not been tested in human trials."
- **Don't say:** "Muscle growth", "hyperplasia", any comparison to Increlex.
- **Store-page note:** No vial size listed.

## 17. EPITALON
- **Aliases:** epithalon, epithalamin (the older pineal extract), AEDG.
- **Class:** Bioregulator tetrapeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly).
- **What it is:** A lab-made peptide of four amino acids, a synthetic version of a pineal-gland extract studied in Russia.
- **Mechanism:** Claimed to affect telomerase (an enzyme that maintains chromosome ends) and melatonin rhythms. Cell studies show telomerase effects; human proof is lacking.
- **Evidence:** Rungs 4 to 5. Mostly small human studies and animal work from one research group (Khavinson), with little independent replication. A 2025 independent review (Araj et al., IJMS) is the best summary [S49].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1].
- **US:** Removed from Category 2 April 2026; PCAC voted 7-5 (one source says 7-4) to recommend on 2026-07-24; not final [S36, S37].
- **Clinician screens for:** cancer history (telomerase effects [VERIFY rationale]); unknown human safety.
- **Say:** "Epitalon is a lab-made peptide of four amino acids. Human research is limited and mostly from one group."
- **Don't say:** "Telomere lengthening", "longevity", "reverses aging", "life extension".

## 18. MOTS-c
- **Aliases:** MOTS, MOTSc.
- **Class:** Mitochondrial-derived peptide (16 amino acids [VERIFY]).
- **What it is:** A peptide the body makes from instructions in its mitochondria (the cell's energy makers).
- **Mechanism:** In animal studies it acts on muscle metabolism and insulin sensitivity.
- **Evidence:** Rung 4, and only for an analogue. CohBar's analogue CB4211 had a 4-week phase 1b in 20 people with obesity and fatty liver (NCT03998514): well tolerated, with exploratory liver-enzyme and glucose changes, company-reported only. No trials of native MOTS-c in people [S50].
- **Approved anywhere:** No.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1].
- **US:** Removed from Category 2 April 2026; PCAC voted 7-5 (2 abstentions) to recommend; not final [S36, S37].
- **Clinician screens for:** diabetes and its medicines; unknown human safety.
- **Say:** "MOTS-c is a peptide the body makes in its mitochondria. Lab-made versions haven't been approved or properly tested in people."
- **Don't say:** "Exercise in a vial", "mitochondrial booster", "metabolic reset".

## 19. SS-31 (ELAMIPRETIDE)
- **Aliases:** elamipretide, Forzinity, Bendavia [VERIFY], MTP-131 [VERIFY].
- **Class:** Mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide.
- **What it is:** Elamipretide, which binds to a fat (cardiolipin) in the inner mitochondrial membrane [VERIFY].
- **Evidence:** Rung 2. FDA granted accelerated approval to Stealth BioTherapeutics' Forzinity on 2025-09-19, the first treatment for Barth syndrome (a rare genetic disease), based on muscle strength in the TAZPOWER trial. A confirmatory trial is required [S51]. Other indications did not reach approval [VERIFY].
- **Approved anywhere:** US only, for Barth syndrome.
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory [S1]. Not authorized [VERIFY].
- **US:** FDA's August 2026 Royal Peptides letter named "SS-31 (Elamipretide)" as an unapproved new drug when sold as a research product [S25]. Forzinity is the only lawful US product.
- **Clinician screens for:** whether the person has the labelled condition at all; kidney function [VERIFY]; injection-site reactions (most common label side effect) [S51].
- **Say:** "SS-31, also called elamipretide, is approved in the US only for a rare genetic condition called Barth syndrome."
- **Don't say:** "Mitochondrial repair", "energy", "anti-aging", "FDA-approved" without the narrow indication.

## 20. NAD+
- **Aliases:** NAD, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide.
- **Class:** Not a peptide. A coenzyme in every living cell.
- **What it is:** A molecule cells use to make energy and repair DNA. Levels fall with age in animal studies.
- **Evidence:** Rung 4 for injection or IV. A 2019 pilot measured blood levels during a six-hour infusion. A 2026 retrospective at a wellness clinic found IV NAD+ caused moderate to severe stomach symptoms, faster heart rate and chest pressure during infusions. Most evidence is for oral precursors, not IV [S52].
- **Approved anywhere:** Not as an injectable drug for wellness [VERIFY].
- **Canada:** Named in Health Canada's 2026 advisory (injectable) [S1].
- **US:** Compounding status [VERIFY with counsel]; marketing claims about "energy" or "anti-aging" are drug claims.
- **Clinician screens for:** heart conditions; blood pressure; pregnancy; cancer history [VERIFY rationale]; infusion tolerance.
- **Say:** "NAD+ is a coenzyme found in every living cell. It isn't a peptide."
- **Don't say:** "Cellular energy", "brain fog cure", "reverse aging", "detox", "addiction treatment".

## 21. GLUTATHIONE
- **Aliases:** GSH, "gluta", reduced glutathione.
- **Class:** Tripeptide antioxidant (glutamate, cysteine, glycine).

## 22. SEMAX

- **Say:** "Semax is a lab-made peptide developed in Russia, where it is registered as a medicine. It isn't approved in Canada or the US."
- **Don't say:** "Nootropic", "focus", "neuroprotective", "BDNF booster".

## 25. GHK-Cu

## CROSS-CATALOGUE NOTES
- **Delist candidates** (no lawful or clinician-supportable route, or highest risk): Retatrutide, Melanotan II, HGH (research vial), Bacteriostatic water (shelf sale), and the named blends until contents and counsel review are done. This is a recommendation for Grace and counsel, not a legal ruling.
- **Approved-active-ingredient products** (tirzepatide, tesamorelin, PT-141, oxytocin, HGH, SS-31): the approval belongs to the branded product and its indication. A compounded or research version is never "the same", "generic" or "FDA-approved".
- **Tested athletes:** WADA's S0 bans any substance without government approval for human therapeutic use, so every unapproved product above is prohibited for them at all times [S30].
- **Purity:** FDA's 2026 letters did not confirm that research products even contained what the label said [S25]. A COA is one lab's report on one lot, not approval.
