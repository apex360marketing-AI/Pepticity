# TASTE JUDGE

Owner, 9 Nov 2026: "Judge taste properly. If no existing skill lets you tell nicer from not-nicer,
what people like vs not, unique vs generic, create one first." `award-jury-review` scores quality,
`anti-slop-gate` blocks slop, `site-elevation-audit` lists upgrades. This skill answers three
different questions: **Is it nicer than the reference? Will people like it on first sight? Is it ours?**

## 1. What people like (the research this rests on)
- First impressions form in about 50 ms and hold (Lindgaard et al. 2006). Judge the TOP SCREEN first, at a glance, before reading.
- The best-liked pages pair LOW visual complexity with HIGH prototypicality (Tuch et al. 2012; Reinecke et al. 2013): people like a page that looks clearly like its category, done with fewer, better things. Busy + unfamiliar scores worst.
- Beautiful reads as easier to use (aesthetic-usability effect, Kurosu and Kashimura 1995). Polish is not decoration; it is perceived usability.
- Fluency: things that are easy to process feel truer and nicer (Reber et al. 2004). One focal point per screen, strong contrast, plain words.
- MAYA: "most advanced, yet acceptable" (Loewy). One unexpected signature per page, everything else familiar.
- Luxury cues (Hagtvedt and Patrick; luxury retail practice): restraint, negative space, slow motion, few colours, large type-scale contrast, real material (metal, glass, photo), product as hero.

## 2. Measured proxies (run on 1440x900 and 390x844 screenshots, every scroll stop)
| Proxy | Nicer | Not nicer |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct hues on screen (excluding greys) | 1-2 (metal + one glow) | 3+ |
| Focal points per screen (things competing for the eye) | 1, at most 2 | 3+ |
| Primary CTAs per screen | 1 (+1 ghost) | 2+ filled |
| Ink coverage (non-ground pixels) | 18-40% | over 55% (busy) or under 8% with nothing to look at (empty) |
| Type-scale ratio, headline to body | 4x or more | under 2.5x |
| Motion speed of moving pieces | under 600 px/s, eases 0.9-1.8 s | snaps, flicks, under 0.4 s entrances |
| Scroll per reveal | one idea per screen, held | several things appear at once |
| Gaps | one rhythm (multiples of the base) | mixed, uneven gaps |
| Alignment | one centre axis or a named grid | near-misses of 2-8 px |
Record numbers, not adjectives. A screen failing 3+ proxies is "not nicer" whatever it looks like at first.

## 3. Nicer than the reference (pairwise, never absolute)
1. Put each screen next to the matching reference screen: winery (feel: calm, one shape language, two fonts, slow fixed-pace scenes), 9-gold (ticket, chrome headline, gold), 10-platinum (lens buttons, glass slab, floors).
2. For each pair answer: which would a stranger pick in 3 seconds, and why in one line (calmer, clearer focal point, richer material, better type contrast, more product).
3. The new page must win or tie on at least 80% of pairs. Every loss becomes a fix item with the exact screen.

## 4. Unique vs generic (the swap test)
- Logo swap: replace PEPTICITY with any other brand name. If the page still fits any brand, it is generic. Our signs: the real PEPTICITY vial, the PEPTICITY font with depth, the cut-corner/hex/ticket shape language, chrome + UV on matte black.
- Template smell (from `award-jury-review` section 5) counts here too: centred hero + three equal cards + logo strip; default fonts; same fade-up on everything; flat rounded rectangles.
- Signature: name the one moment someone would screen-record. None = generic.
- Colourway pairs: two colourways must differ in at least 4 of: scroll moment, effect codes, hover, menu, hero composition, card shape. A recolour fails.

## 5. Selling taste (for store pages)
- Product visible in the first screen, photoreal, lit.
- Price, size and add-to-cart readable without hunting; the buy path never needs more than one scroll.
- Every capture hook looks like part of the design (same glass, same shapes), never a bolted-on popup look.
- Calm beats loud: no countdowns, no flashing, no fake scarcity (also the law, see `honest-urgency-and-social-proof`).

## 6. Procedure
1. Screenshots: 1440 and 390, top plus every major scroll stop, plus hover of the main CTA and a card.
2. Fresh eyes: a reviewer subagent gets ONLY the screenshots, the reference screenshots and this file. It returns pairwise picks, proxy numbers and the weakest screen.
3. Merge with your own read. Fix every loss and every proxy failure, re-shoot, re-judge.
4. Report as: `TASTE: wins x/y vs reference · proxies failed: … · swap test: pass/fail · signature: … · weakest screen: … (fixed / not fixed)`.

## Never
- Never call something "premium" or "nicer" without the pairwise pick and the numbers.
- Never fix taste by adding more (more glow, more effects, more cards). Taste fixes usually remove.
