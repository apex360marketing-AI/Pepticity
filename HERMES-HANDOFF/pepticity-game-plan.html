Top 5 buyers

Ranked on 2025–2026 market data. The two groups who say they're "not feeling well" or "low on energy" are served through the 55+ and women 40+ paths and an "energy" path. "Fatigue" is a symptom the assessment asks about, never a promise the site makes.

1 · Women 40–60

The largest group: women are the majority of GLP-1 users, and use peaks at 50–64. They want to be listened to, a clear all-in price, and named women clinicians.

Avoid: shrinking-body imagery, bro aesthetics.

2 · Healthy aging 55+

The highest disposable income. 73% are actively working on their health. They want a phone number a human answers, a medication review and plain English.

Avoid: hype, countdown timers, tiny grey fine print.

3 · Men 35–55

Data-driven cash payers in the same market as Hims and Maximus. They want labs before anything is prescribed, named clinicians and pharmacy sourcing.

Avoid: "gains" talk, supplement-store design.

4 · Training & recovery

The likeliest group to buy grey market today. Win them with "real pharmacy, real prescription" and lab proof. This group carries the highest compliance risk: no dosing content ever.

Channels: coaches, gyms, podcasts.

5 · Weight & metabolic

12% of US adults use a GLP-1. They're burned by bait pricing. Lead with an all-in price lock, and never claim equivalence to Wegovy or Zepbound.

This group brings people in; keep them with longevity care.

Compliance rules every version follows

Never

  • Dosing, cycles, stacks, calculators, reconstitution
  • Syringes, bacteriostatic water, injection images
  • "Heals", "reverses", "boosts", "may help", "studied for"
  • Fake reviews, star ratings, before/after, invented stats
  • Saying a compounded drug equals an approved one

Always

  • "A licensed clinician decides if a plan is right for you"
  • The full price shown before payment; cancel in two clicks
  • Sample data labelled SAMPLE; offers in [brackets] until real
  • A footer disclaimer, approved by counsel
  • A dated screenshot of every published page

This is a risk scan, not legal advice. Grace's counsel makes the final call.

Peptide research, in brief

  • FDA, 2026: repeated warning letters to "research use only" sellers (Mar, Jun and Aug 2026). The FDA judges the whole website, and it cited dosing info, calculators, testimonials and bundled bacteriostatic water. A research-use label gives no protection.
  • Compounding opening up: in April 2026 the FDA removed 12 peptides from its restricted list. In July 2026 its advisory committee voted to recommend 6 for compounding (including BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, Epitalon and Semax). Nothing is final, so check the 503A list before launch.
  • Competition: Hims & Hers plans to add peptides by year-end. Peptide Sciences shut down in March 2026. The gap is a premium, design-led brand that pairs lab-grade transparency with compliant care.
  • Canada: Health Canada treats injectable peptides as prescription drugs, has seized products and won a court injunction. Canadian sales need a prescription framework; cosmetic topical peptides are the safe lane.
  • Ads & payments: Google and Meta require LegitScript for health and prescription ads. Stripe restricts peptide sellers. Plan on SEO, email, SMS and affiliates first.
