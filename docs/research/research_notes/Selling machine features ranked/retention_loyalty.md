## Q1. Evidence base: what lift can each feature claim, and where do the famous numbers really come from?

### Takeaway
The "5% retention = 25–95% profit" line comes from Bain/Reichheld. It started as "25% to 85%" in the 1990 HBR article "Zero Defections"; the 25–95% wording turns up later, and Ipsos says it depends heavily on a firm's starting retention rate. The strongest independent social-proof number is Spiegel Research Center (2017): a product with five reviews is 270% more likely to be bought than one with none. Subscription churn benchmarks are vendor-sourced and conflict with each other. Wellness is reported as the highest-churn category, at roughly 8–9% a month.

### Cited Findings
- **Bain/Reichheld origin [ACADEMIC/INDEPENDENT, HBR 1990]:** Reichheld & W. Earl Sasser, "Zero Defections: Quality Comes to Services," HBR 1990. Wording: "by reducing customer defection by a mere 5%, companies can boost profits by 25% to 85%." Sources: [Forrester blog citing it](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/profiting-from-post-sale-marketing); [Goodreads note (Hacking Growth)](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/34220175-hacking-growth/4381559-gareth/2ca56bb8-48fb-43d7-8e4a-7b28c8ccb495)
- **The "25% to 95%" wording:** Reported as appearing in Amy Gallo, "The Value of Keeping the Right Customers," HBR, 29 Oct 2014, which cites Reichheld/Bain. Reichheld's own LinkedIn piece frames it as a 5% retention increase lifting *lifetime cash flow per customer* by 25–95%. That is not the same as company profit. Sources: [Reichheld, "A History of Customer Love in Three Acts" (LinkedIn mirror)](https://meilu.jpshuntong.com/url-68747470733a2f2f7777772e6c696e6b6564696e2e636f6d/pulse/history-customer-love-three-acts-fred-reichheld); [Zenify summary](https://zenify.vn/blog/retention-profit-impact)
- **Critique [INDEPENDENT, research firm]:** Ipsos argues the claim is overstated: "the ability to generate additional profits by improving retention is highly contingent upon any firm's current retention rate." Source: [Ipsos, Shattering the Myths of Customer Loyalty](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/shattering-myths-customer-loyalty)
- **"Prescription for Cutting Costs" (Bain, 2001):** I could not confirm this title as the source in this session. See Gaps.
- **Spiegel Research Center [ACADEMIC, Northwestern Medill, 2017]:** "Purchase likelihood for a product with five reviews is 270% greater than the purchase likelihood of a product with no reviews." Returns diminish after a point. Data: more than 100,000 SKUs and 15 million review page views from two CPG retailers and one high-end gift retailer over a year. This is relative purchase likelihood, not a 270% rise in sales. Source: [Spiegel Research Center, How Online Reviews Influence Sales](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/How-Online-Reviews-Influence-Sales); [eBook PDF](https://www.xait.com/hubfs/Spiegel_Online-Review_eBook_Jun2017_FINAL.pdf)
- **Subscription churn, Recharge 2026 [VENDOR BENCHMARK, via third parties; the original report was not retrieved]:**
  - Wellness has the highest churn of any category, at 8.8% a month. [RetainUp summary of Recharge 2026 Trend Report](https://retainup.co/recharge-subscription-trend-report-2026/)
  - Median monthly churn: beauty/personal care 8.4%, pets 8.1%, food/bev 7.7%, home 3.9%. [SubJolt guide, attributed to Recharge 2026](https://www.subjolt.com/guides/dtc-subscription-retention/)
  - About 61% of voluntary cancellations happen at the first two renewals. [RetainUp](https://retainup.co/recharge-subscription-trend-report-2026/)
  - Older Recharge figures, widely cited: about 45% of subscribers retained at 6 months and about 33% at 12 months. [Prooflytics](https://prooflytics.io/blog/recharge-marketing-analytics)
  - 2024 Recharge/SubSummit panel: 7.1% monthly churn, made up of 4.1% voluntary and 3.0% involuntary. [eightx DTC churn index](https://eightx.co/blog/dtc-subscription-churn-index-2026)
- **Recurly 2026 [VENDOR BENCHMARK]:** Covers 76M subscribers and 2,200 merchants. 52% of consumers cancelled at least one subscription in the past year for lack of use. Source: [Recurly State of Subscriptions](https://recurly.com/content/state-of-subscriptions-report/)
- **Generic "8–15% monthly e-commerce churn":** This is a directional figure from churn-calculator sites with no primary data behind it. Do not use it as fact. [ChurnCost](https://churncost.com/ecommerce)

### Inferences
- Use the original 1990 framing ("25–85%, defection-based, service firms") or say "Bain research suggests large profit sensitivity to retention". Do not quote "25–95%" as a universal fact.
- With wellness churn at about 8.8% a month, roughly two-thirds of new subscribers would be gone within 12 months. That matches the older 33% 12-month retention figure. Cancellation-save flows aimed at renewals 1–2 matter most.
- The Spiegel finding says the first five reviews matter most, so collecting early reviews has more value than building a large review volume.

### Gaps
- McKinsey and Bond Brand Loyalty reports (the "Loyalty Report"): not retrieved in this session, so no figures are given. The report writer should not cite them without a fresh lookup.
- I could not obtain the Recharge 2026 report directly. All category churn figures are second-hand.
- No independent (non-vendor) lift data was found for loyalty points, referrals, Q&A, VIP drops, wishlists or gift cards. All lift claims for those features are vendor or industry claims.

In the US, click-to-cancel was vacated in July 2025, removed from the CFR in February 2026, and reopened as an ANPRM in March 2026. Enforcement continues under ROSCA and Section 5 of the FTC Act.

  - Enforcement continues under ROSCA, FTC Act s.5 and state auto-renewal laws. The NextMed complaint is cited as applying the vacated rule's standards through s.5. [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses)

### Inferences
- **Build on a static site:** recurring billing through the payment processor, plus a self-serve "manage / skip / cancel" page and pre-renewal reminder emails.
- **Default compliance design (Canada-first, US-safe):**
  - express opt-in checkbox at checkout
  - clear renewal terms
  - reminder before each renewal
  - online cancel in the same medium the customer signed up in, with no forced call
  - This meets the strictest version (vacated FTC / proposed Ontario) and future-proofs the flow.
- **Pharmacy inducement risk:** if any SKU is prescription-only, a subscribe-and-save *discount* on it may count as a pharmacy inducement under provincial college rules. That would need counsel review (provincial bylaws not researched here).
- **Platform risk:** peptide merchants face processor screening (see the LegitScript guide). Confirm the processor accepts the catalogue before building auto-ship.

- I did not retrieve FTC Federal Register primary documents. Dates are from law-firm commentary.

## Q4. Referrals: tools, pricing, CASL referral exemption

### Takeaway
ReferralCandy publishes pricing: a monthly fee plus a success fee on referred sales. Friendbuy and Talkable are quote-only with enterprise minimums. CASL's referral exemption covers only the *first* message, only where the referrer has a qualifying relationship, and the message must name the referrer and say it was sent because of the referral.

### Cited Findings
- **ReferralCandy [aggregator, citing official pricing]:**
  - Basic $39/mo + 10.5% success fee
  - Grow $79/mo + 3.5%
  - Scale $249/mo + 1.5%
  - Enterprise $799/mo + 0.25%
  - Toolradar (updated 27 Jan 2026) listed $59/mo with a 14-day trial, a conflicting figure.
  - [StackScored referral pricing](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/referral-marketing/); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/referralcandy/pricing)
- **Friendbuy / Talkable:** custom quotes, with implementation fees and 12-month minimums reported. An estimated $500–$3,000/mo for mid-market is an aggregator estimate, not a published price. [StackScored Friendbuy vs Talkable](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/referral-marketing/compare/friendbuy-vs-talkable/); [Rivo's Talkable review (competitor source)](https://www.rivo.io/blog/talkable-review); [Capterra Talkable](https://www.capterra.com/p/152418/talkable/)
- **CASL referral exemption [PRIMARY REG]:** Electronic Commerce Protection Regulations SOR/2013-221, s.4(1). Paragraph 6(1)(a) of CASL (consent) "does not apply to the first commercial electronic message that is sent by a person" to contact an individual "following a referral by any individual" who has a qualifying relationship (existing business, non-business, family or personal) with the sender or recipient, as summarised from the regulation. The message must "disclose the full name of the individual or individuals who made the referral" and state it was sent as a result of the referral. [Justice Laws SOR/2013-221](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2013-221/page-1.html)
- **s.3(b) exclusion [PRIMARY REG]:** a CEM "sent in response to a request, inquiry or complaint or is otherwise solicited" is excluded from s.6. [Justice Laws SOR/2013-221](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2013-221/page-1.html)

### Inferences
- **Safest build:** referrers share a personal link themselves (copy link / native share), so the referrer is the sender of a personal message. The brand emails the friend only after the friend signs up with consent. If the brand sends one message on referral, the s.4(1) conditions apply: one message only, the referrer's full name, and a referral statement.
- ReferralCandy's 10.5% success fee on the Basic tier is costly at low volume. An in-house referral code with a server-side ledger is simple to build on a static site with a serverless function.
- **Reward design:** cash or discounts for referring purchases of Rx items may count as pharmacy inducements. Prefer access or status rewards (early drops) and keep rewards off Rx SKUs (counsel to confirm).

### Gaps
- The exact list of relationship types in s.4(1)(a)–(b) was summarised rather than quoted in full. Read the full text before finalising copy.
- No published Friendbuy or Talkable prices. Non-Shopify support was not confirmed for any referral vendor.
- No independent referral lift data was retrieved.

## Q5. Reviews with photos/UGC and product Q&A: tools, pricing, peptide compliance, moderation

### Takeaway
For a custom site, REVIEWS.io and Stamped are the multi-platform choices. Judge.me and Okendo are framed as Shopify-only, and Trustpilot is mainly a company-review product. In Canada, reviews are "representations" under the Competition Act. Undisclosed employee reviews have been flagged by the Competition Bureau, and penalties can reach $10M ($15M for repeat violations) or three times the benefit. Health-product ad guidelines limit claims to those Health Canada has authorized. A peptide store can probably show reviews limited to service, shipping, packaging and experience, and must moderate out efficacy and dosing claims; I found no regulator text approving this directly (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings
- **Judge.me:** free plan, or a flat $15/mo paid plan. Described as Shopify-only: "if you ever migrate platforms or run a headless storefront, you'll need to rebuild your review stack." [Wiserreview Judge.me review](https://wiserreview.com/blog/judge-me-review/)
- **Okendo:** order-based pricing from about $19–$29. Free up to 50 orders/mo. Shopify-native. [Ringly, Okendo alternatives](https://www.ringly.io/blog/okendo-alternatives); [Idukki](https://idukki.io/blog/best-shopify-review-apps-2026)
- **Stamped:** prices conflict, $23/mo vs a $199/mo reviews-only entry. Free plan dropped. Works with Shopify, BigCommerce and Magento. [Stamped blog (vendor)](https://website.stamped.io/blog/the-shopify-brands-guide-to-reviews-platforms-2026/); [Idukki](https://idukki.io/blog/best-shopify-review-apps-2026)
- **REVIEWS.io:** supports "Shopify, Magento, WooCommerce, and custom headless builds." Entry around $19–$29. [Signed Reviews](https://signedreviews.com/blog/trustpilot-alternatives-for-ecommerce/)
- **Trustpilot:** product reviews are an add-on with no public pricing. Premium is about $599/mo and excludes product reviews. [Aissist](https://aissist.io/insights/trustpilot-alternatives)
- **Yotpo Reviews:** no 2026 pricing retrieved.
- **Competition Act and reviews [legal commentary]:** reviews and testimonials are representations made "by any means whatever." The Bureau warned that employees must disclose their employment when reviewing their employer or its competitors. Civil penalties up to the greater of $10M ($15M subsequent) or 3x the benefit. [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/knowledge/publications/41049288/fake-reviews-competition-bureau-warns-businesses-of-stiff-penalties-associated-with-online-reviews). The latest amendments received royal assent on 20 June 2024 (same source). It is not confirmed whether they changed the review rules. The Bureau opened a fake-review inquiry into Amazon on 9 Nov 2021. [Cartt](https://cartt.ca/competition-bureau-going-after-fake-reviews-again/). Incentivized reviews fall under Bureau purview according to industry commentary (an advocacy source). [BrandSpark](https://www.brandspark.com/?p=23397)
- **Health-product advertising:**
  - Ad Standards' Guidelines for Consumer Advertising of Health Products were updated in January 2018 with Health Canada and cover non-Rx drugs, NHPs and devices. Preclearance approves only claims authorized by Health Canada. [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2018/ad-standards-publishes-guidelines-for-consumer-adv); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/food-and-drugs-law/679074/ad-standards-publishes-guidelines-for-consumer-advertising-of-health-products)
  - For professional-facing ads, PAAB says non-evidence-based testimonials are not acceptable. [PAAB Code](https://code.paab.ca/making-statements.htm)
- **Health Canada guidance** distinguishing advertising from other activities for health products exists. A brand displaying and curating reviews is likely to be treated as advertising (inference). [Mondaq on HC guidance](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/healthcare/1361220/health-canada-publishes-guidance-on-distinction-between-advertising-and-other-activities-for-health-products)

### Inferences
- **Moderation rules to adopt (my inference from the above, not regulator text):**
  1. Verified buyers only.
  2. Allow service, shipping, packaging, purity documentation and customer-care comments.
  3. Reject or redact any mention of outcomes, conditions, body changes, doses or protocols.
  4. Publish negative reviews on the same rules. Selective suppression is misleading under the Competition Act.
  5. No reward conditional on a positive review. If any reward is given for leaving a review, disclose it on the review.
  6. Employees and affiliates must disclose.
  7. Photos: product and packaging only, no before/after body images.
- For Rx peptides, C.01.044 limits Rx advertising to name, price and quantity (per the brief and the peptide-regulatory skill). Showing product-level reviews on Rx SKUs is high-risk. Store-level (service) reviews are lower risk.
- **Q&A:** answer only from vetted copy (availability, storage of the sealed product, shipping, lab documents). Route dosing or "will it help me" questions to "speak with a licensed clinician." In-house build: a static JSON of moderated Q&A.
- **Build options on a static site:** the REVIEWS.io widget and API, or an in-house review store (serverless + DB) with a verified-order token emailed after delivery.

### Gaps
- I found no Canadian regulator text specifically addressing customer reviews on unauthorized or Rx peptide product pages. This is the key open legal question for counsel.
- The text of the Competition Bureau's own current reviews guidance was not retrieved.
- Acceptable-use or content policies for Judge.me, Yotpo, Okendo, Stamped, Trustpilot and REVIEWS.io were not checked for peptide or unapproved-drug bans. Trustpilot's guidelines in particular should be read before use.

## Q6. Gift cards and wishlists

### Takeaway
In BC, most gift cards cannot expire. A card sold with an expiry date is treated as having none. The only permitted fees are for personalization and for replacing a lost or stolen card, and terms must be disclosed. No independent lift data was found for wishlists; they are a simple in-house build.

### Cited Findings
- **BC [PRIMARY LAW + regulator]:**
  - Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act, Part 4.1, and the Prepaid Purchase Cards Regulation. Cards sold with an expiry date are treated as having none.
  - Exceptions: cards for a specific good or service, and store credit issued on a return or cancellation of a non-refundable purchase.
  - Permitted fees: personalization and replacement of lost or stolen cards.
  - Restrictions, fees and how to check the balance must be disclosed.
  - [BC Laws, BPCPA](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/lc/statreg/04002_05); [Consumer Protection BC, Gift cards](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/consumer-help/consumer-information-gift-cards/); [Lawson Lundell](https://lawsonlundell.com/the-business-law-blog/gift-cards-legislation-gifts-that-keep-on-giving)
- Consumer Protection BC jurisdiction applies if the consumer lives in BC *or* the business is located in BC. [Consumer Protection BC](https://www.consumerprotectionbc.ca/consumer-help/consumer-information-gift-cards/)

### Inferences
- Because PEPTICITY is BC-based, issue gift cards with no expiry and no dormancy fees, nationwide. That also meets other provinces' no-expiry regimes (other provinces not verified here).
- A gift card redeemable on Rx items could be argued to be an inducement if it is given free as a reward. Sold gift cards are lower risk. Promotional free credit tied to Rx should be checked by counsel.
- **Wishlist build:** browser localStorage for guests, plus an emailed "save my list" link (which also captures a lead with CASL consent). Back-in-stock or price-drop emails sent from a wishlist need express consent unless they are transactional.

### Gaps
- Ontario, Quebec and Alberta gift card rules were not retrieved.
- No independent or academic lift data for wishlists or gift cards.
- Whether promotional gift cards count as gift cards under the BC regulation (versus free promotional cards) was not confirmed.
