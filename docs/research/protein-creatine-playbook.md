## ONE-PAGE SUMMARY

**The idea**
- Add PROTEIN and CREATINE to PEPTICITY as shelves of their own.
- Show each product separate, then one slick combined view.
- Aim copy at adults 40+ / 55+ who may not know these products exist for them (owner's point).

**What we found**
- Creatine in Canada is a natural health product (NHP). It needs a product licence (NPN) and a site-licensed maker or importer. Sources: [Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/04/creatine-market-entry-canada/).
- Protein powder in Canada can be a food OR an NHP, depending on how it is described and the claims on it. Sources: [Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/09/protein-powder-regulations-canada/), [Quality Smart Solutions](https://qualitysmartsolutions.com/blog/food-nhp-compliance/protein-supplements-vs-protein-food-in-canada-understanding-health-canadas-classification-rules/).
- The "easy" US dropship apps (Supliful, Productifi) list the United States only as their sourcing location. Neither page mentions Canada. Sources: [Supliful](https://apps.shopify.com/supliful), [Productifi](https://apps.shopify.com/productifi).
- That matters: a finished NHP brought into Canada must go through a site-licensed importer ([Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/04/creatine-market-entry-canada/)). So "US dropship straight to a Canadian customer" is a counsel question, not a plug-and-play.
- Canadian options found: Thrive Protein (resale, 5-unit minimum), plus site-licensed Ontario makers (NutraNorth, NutraLab, Nutriza) with no minimums published.

**Recommended path (see section 4)**

- Resell a Canadian brand first only if the owner wants something on the shelf sooner.

## 3. CANADIAN RULES IN PLAIN WORDS

**Mark: COUNSEL MUST CONFIRM everything in this section.** These are secondary sources (consultancies), not Health Canada text.

### How each product is classed
- CREATINE
  - Regulated as an NHP ingredient. Not permitted in conventional or supplemented foods at present: [Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/04/creatine-market-entry-canada/).
  - Needs an NPN (product licence number) before sale.
  - Route: Class I licence is the fastest when the product matches the monograph. Class II when mixed with other ingredients. Class III when outside the monograph.
  - Timelines on that page: 60 / 90 / 310+ days for Class I / II / III. Their estimate, not a promise.
  - Daily dose range on that page: 3 g to 20 g by directions. Confirm against the real monograph.
  - Official database check: licensed creatine NHPs exist, for example a 5.0 g creatine monohydrate powder with an active licence: [Natural Factors licence](https://health-products.canada.ca/lnhpd-bdpsnh/info?licence=80125910); and a creatine plus glutamine workout powder: [licence](https://health-products.canada.ca/lnhpd-bdpsnh/info?licence=80128227). Licence wording on those pages is theirs, never to be copied as PEPTICITY copy.
  - Workout supplements are an official NHP monograph (Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate). The monograph page itself was not read: confirm dose and risk statements there.
  - Whether creatine is on Health Canada's List of Permitted Supplemental Ingredients (food route): the official guidance excerpt read did not mention it, and a consultancy says it is not permitted. Treat as NOT food until confirmed with the Food Directorate (smiu-ugdi@hc-sc.gc.ca is the contact on their page).
  - Confirm with counsel: which monograph applies, and exact wording allowed.
- PROTEIN POWDER (OFFICIAL HEALTH CANADA TEXT, ADDED IN THE AUDIT)
  - Health Canada's own guidance says representation is the main factor; format and ingredients alone do not decide it. Words or pictures such as "muscle gains", "muscle building", "increased protein synthesis" point to NHP. A shake, drink or meal-replacement presentation, sold among ordinary foods, points to food: [Classification at the food / NHP interface](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/classification-products-at-food-natural-health-product-interface.html).
  - The Supplemented Foods guidance lists protein dry mixes to be reconstituted as a food category but excludes "protein dry mixes marketed as workout supplements with recommended directions of use", which it says are NHPs under the Workout Supplements monograph: [Supplemented Foods Regulations guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/supplemented-foods-regulations.html).
  - Site rule that follows: on the PEPTICITY pages, no muscle-building words or pictures, no scoop-and-dose directions in copy, until counsel decides the route. Classification is case by case and changes if labels or claims change.
  - Secondary sources (consultancies) below agree with this; kept for context.
- PROTEIN POWDER (SECONDARY SOURCES)
  - It depends on the full product profile: composition, format, how it is represented, claims. "A scoop, serving size, or added vitamin does not independently determine the pathway": [Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/09/protein-powder-regulations-canada/).
  - If it carries health claims such as muscle building, it is classed as an NHP and needs an NPN: [Quality Smart Solutions](https://qualitysmartsolutions.com/blog/food-nhp-compliance/protein-supplements-vs-protein-food-in-canada-understanding-health-canadas-classification-rules/).
  - A plain protein powder with no therapeutic claims may be a food.
  - If it is sold as a meal replacement or nutritional supplement, extra food rules (Division 24) apply: [CFIA page](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/industry/foods-special-dietary-use).
  - Supplemented foods are a separate category with their own table and caution marks: [Health Canada overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/supplemented-foods.html), [guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/supplemented-foods-regulations.html).
  - A product cannot be both food and NHP.
  - Confirm with counsel: which route each PEPTICITY protein takes, and whether our shelf wording moves it into NHP.

### Labelling
- Food route: common name, net quantity, responsible party name and address, ingredients, allergens, Nutrition Facts table, English and French. Front-of-package symbol applies when sugar, saturated fat or sodium thresholds are crossed (in force 1 Jan 2026): [Health Canada guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/front-package-nutrition-symbol-labelling-industry.html).
- NHP route: label must match the licence (NPN, medicinal ingredients, purpose, directions, risk statements).
- Counsel to confirm: bilingual rules and exact risk statements for creatine.

### Allowed claims
- NHP claims must match the licence and monograph. Claims outside it can need a different, slower licence.
- On the site: no health or medical claims. Describe what the product is, what is in it, how it is tested. Nothing about outcomes.
- Counsel to confirm: any wording for adults 40+/55+. Avoid "sarcopenia", "ageing muscle", "fall prevention", "bone health".

### Selling online
- NHP: anyone who makes, packages, labels or imports needs a site licence. For foreign-made NHPs, either the foreign site holds a Canadian licence or the Canadian importer does: [Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/04/creatine-market-entry-canada/), [Nutraingredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2014/12/18/Overcoming-the-hurdles-of-importing-nutraceuticals-and-cosmeceuticals-into-Canada).
- Food protein imports may need a Safe Food for Canadians licence: [Sourcenutra](https://sourcenutra.com/2026/09/protein-powder-regulations-canada/).
- Product recall history exists: Health Canada suspended a creatine licence after contamination was found: [Health Canada notice](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/product-licence-suspended-perfect-sports-core-series-pure-creatine-all-lots-recalled). This is why testing and batch records matter.
- Site rules already in the repo still apply: CASL consent, no fake urgency, all-in pricing, no bundles by health goal (`docs/research/lead-magnet-hooks.md`).

### OPTION 2: PRIVATE LABEL WITH A CANADIAN MANUFACTURER
- PROS
  - PEPTICITY on the bottle.
  - Site-licensed Canadian makers can carry the licensing work (to confirm in writing).
  - Keeps imports and cross-border duties out of the picture.
- CONS
  - Minimums and lead times unknown until quotes arrive.
  - Creatine needs an NPN: the licence step takes time (60 to 310+ days per class, per Sourcenutra).
  - Needs counsel and real label work.
- STEPS
  1. Send the same 10-question brief to NutraNorth, NutraLab, Nutriza (and True North for whey).
  2. Counsel confirms food vs NHP per product.
  3. Order samples; check testing certificates; check the NSF or Informed Sport listings if sport-certification is wanted.
  4. Approve label (owner OK before print).
  5. Small first run; orders ship from the maker or a 3PL after counsel confirms who is the importer and seller.

### OPTION 3: FULL OWN BRAND LATER
- PROS
  - Full control of formula, flavour, price.
  - Can add Creapure or a custom blend.
- CONS
  - Slowest and costliest. Own licences, own quality system, larger minimums.
  - Not the place to start.
- STEPS
  1. Run Option 1 or 2 first and collect sales and quiz data.
  2. Develop the formula with the maker.
  3. Own NPN applications where required.

### MY RECOMMENDATION
- Option 2 as the target. Contact makers now; nothing is committed.
- Option 1 as a bridge only if the owner wants a shelf live sooner.
- Option 3 only after 6+ months of real order data.
- Reason: the owner wants his own brand ordered from his own site, and the Canadian route avoids the importer problem that blocks the US dropship apps.
