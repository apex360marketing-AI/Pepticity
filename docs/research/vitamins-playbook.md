## 3. CANADIAN RULES IN PLAIN WORDS

All of this is a research summary, not legal advice. **[COUNSEL]** must confirm before launch.

### 3.1 THE LAW IN ONE LINE
- Vitamins and supplements are Natural Health Products (NHPs) under the Natural Health Products Regulations.
- They are regulated by Health Canada's Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD).

### 3.2 PRODUCT LICENCE AND NPN
- Each product needs its own product licence. It gets an 8-digit Natural Product Number (NPN).
- The NPN goes on the principal display panel, with the prefix "NPN". Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/labelling.html
- Review time reported by consultants: about 60 days for simple vitamins, months for complex herbal blends. Source: https://www.superiorsupplementmfg.com/certifications/health-canada-certified-manufacturer/ (consultant claim, **[VERIFY]**)
- Applications go through Health Canada's online portal. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/applications-submissions/product-licensing.html
- A licensed product can be looked up in Health Canada's Licensed Natural Health Products Database. We should check every NPN before it goes on the site.

### 3.3 SITE LICENCE
- Official guidance: a site licence is needed for each physical Canadian site where someone manufactures, packages, labels or imports NHPs for sale.
- Storage alone is not a separate licence activity (importers list their storage buildings).
- Distributors that do not import are exempt from the site licence, but must follow GMP.
- Retailers who import their own product need a licence.
- NOT clearly answered in the guidance: a store that only sells licensed products shipped by others. **[COUNSEL]** must confirm where PEPTICITY falls under each of the 3 start options.
- Decision times in the guidance: 30 days (pre-cleared GMP evidence), 60 days (1 to 9 sites), up to 90 days (10+ sites). No fee stated on that page.
- Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/site-licensing-guidance-document.html
- Rule of thumb: if a licensed maker packs, labels and ships, the licence burden sits with them. **[COUNSEL]**

### 3.4 LABELLING
- Required on the label (official guidance): brand name, NPN, dosage form, net amount; licence holder or importer name; lot and expiry; medicinal ingredients; at least one use; dose; risk information.
- Newer labels use a Product Facts Table.
- Certain information must be in English and French.
- New labelling rules: products licensed on or after 21 June 2025 are exempt from the new requirements under a Ministerial Order; all products have until 22 June 2028 to comply. **[COUNSEL]** to confirm what applies to our SKUs.
- Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/labelling.html

### 3.5 ALLOWED CLAIMS
- Claims must match the product licence (its "terms of market authorization"): the label, the monograph, or the licence scope.
- Describing a product as risk-free, "safe" or "no known side effects" is not acceptable.
- Implying "natural" means safer than a synthetic drug is not acceptable.
- Competition Act also applies: performance claims need adequate and proper testing.
- Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/marketing-drugs-devices/illegal-marketing/health-providers/natural-non-prescription.html
- Not covered by that page: testimonials, disease claims. **[COUNSEL]**
- **PEPTICITY HOUSE RULE: no health or medical claims in any copy.**
  - Product cards show: name, NPN, size, ingredients, price, "view the label".
  - Nothing on benefits, conditions, symptoms, ageing, energy, sleep, weight, joints.
  - Only the exact licensed wording, if counsel approves quoting it at all.
  - The 55+ audience is reached through shelf design and the interest cards, never through claims.

### 3.6 SELLING ONLINE
- The product itself must be licensed, whoever sells it. Health Canada acts mainly on risk and on missing licences.
- Health Canada's retailer sheet (archived, 2009) does not set out specific retailer claim rules. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/information-sheet-1-retailers-informing-yourself-informing-you-about-natural-health-products.html
- Amazon.ca and Canadian fulfilment centres require a valid NPN. Source: https://amzprep.com/how-to-ship-supplements-to-canada/ (vendor blog)
- Also on the list for counsel: CASL consent for emails and texts about vitamins, provincial rules, sales tax treatment of supplements, product liability insurance, recall plan, and who is named as seller.

## 5. THREE WAYS TO START

### 1A. DROPSHIP / READY-NPN BRAND (Recommended)
- What: use a Canadian maker's existing licensed products, sold under the PEPTICITY name (or their label first), and have them pack and ship single orders.
- Pros:
  - Fastest launch. Makers like PURA say private labelling on their NPNs can take about 45 days.
  - Lowest cash. No bulk inventory if dropship is offered.
  - The maker holds the site licence and the GMP burden.
  - Easy to test which products sell.
- Cons:
  - Less brand control. Formulas are theirs.
  - Margin is lower than owning stock.
  - Dropshipping is not confirmed at any maker yet. Synergy has a page, no details.
  - Who holds the NPN needs to be clear.
- Steps:
  - 1. Owner approves outreach (section 2.4 question list).
  - 2. Request quotes from 3 Canadian makers.
  - 3. Counsel confirms the licence and claims position.
  - 4. Pick 6 starter products (section 1.4).
  - 5. Verify each NPN in Health Canada's database.
  - 6. Order samples. Check labels and bilingual tables.
  - 7. Phase 2: build sites/vitamins/ and show Grace.
  - 8. Soft launch with a small shelf.

### 1B. PRIVATE LABEL (own label on a maker's licensed formulas)
- What: the maker produces a batch with the PEPTICITY label. We hold stock (or the maker stores it).
- Pros:
  - Real PEPTICITY brand on the bottle.
  - Better margin per unit.
  - Still uses the maker's licensed formulas and site.
- Cons:
  - MOQ cash up front. Industry range 500 to 5,000 units per SKU (general figures, not quotes).
  - Storage and reorders to manage.
  - Label design, bilingual tables and licence-holder naming need counsel and maker sign-off.
  - Stock risk if a product does not sell.
- Steps:
  - 1. Same as 1A steps 1 to 5.
  - 2. Design the label (needs the owner's brand rules and an OK on one sample first).
  - 3. Maker proofs the label and submits or amends the licence if needed.
  - 4. Place a first batch per SKU.
  - 5. Production 4 to 8 weeks (PURA's figure; others not stated).
  - 6. Receive stock, check lots and expiry.
  - 7. Phase 2 build, then launch.

### 1C. OWN BRAND LATER (custom formulas, own NPNs)
- What: own formulas and own product licences, built over months.
- Pros:
  - Full control and the strongest brand.
  - Best long-term margin.
  - Formulas nobody else sells.
- Cons:
  - Slowest. Product licence reviews take months for complex formulas.
  - Highest cost. Example: PURA lists custom formulations at $800 to $3,000 each, before batches.
  - Needs regulatory help and testing.
  - Owner said "maybe in a month or two", so this misses that timeline.
- Steps:
  - 1. Start 1A first to learn what sells.
  - 2. Pick 1 to 2 winners to formulate.
  - 3. Maker develops the formula.
  - 4. NPN application (maker or consultant).
  - 5. Production and launch after approval.

### RECOMMENDATION
- 1A first. It matches "a month or two", needs the least cash, and gives Grace something real to review.
- Move winners to 1B after 2 to 3 months of sales data.
- Keep 1C as the long game.
