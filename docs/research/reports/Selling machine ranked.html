01Gate zero: who will let us sell

Before any feature matters, a payment company and an email company have to agree to work with the store. On today's policy text, none of the big ones can be assumed to say yes. Every feature in step 3 names which gate it needs.

Biggest finding. The new site has no checkout or payment provider yet (the bot still says "[Payment methods: Grace to confirm]"). Shopify apps mostly don't apply because the site is custom-built, and Shopify Payments takes its banned list from its processor.

PPayments

  • Helcim (Canada) bans "Non-FDA/Health Canada approved peptides". Checked 2 Nov, policy dated 30 Sep 2026. Source
  • Stripe bans "Incorrectly labeled research chemicals" and restricts prescription products sold online. Checked 2 Nov, list dated 22 Sep 2026. Source
  • Card networks have processors scan the website. LegitScript's guide names BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 and ipamorelin as problem peptides and flags a lab-style look. Source
  • High-risk processors aren't a way around it: Corepay says it does "not support research-use-only (RUO) peptide sellers".
  • Apple Pay, Google Pay, one-click upsells, subscriptions and gift cards all run on the processor. If the processor drops the store, they all go at once.

EEmail and text

  • Brevo refuses peptides for human use, "including those labeled 'research use only'". Checked 2 Nov. Source
  • Omnisend bans prescription drugs, "including … drugs for research purposes". Klaviyo and Mailchimp restrict pharmaceutical products.
  • US texting: carriers (via Twilio) reject prescription-drug marketing for good.
  • Get written approval from an email platform before building flows. Keep the list and the flow logic on our side so a ban doesn't wipe them out.

CConsent and counsel

  • CASL: order and shipping messages need no consent. Implied consent lasts 2 years after a purchase and 6 months after an inquiry.
  • Whether starting a checkout counts as an "inquiry" is not settled. Until counsel says yes, put an unticked consent box on every email field.
  • Every feature is only as legal as the product it sells. See step 5.

I KData we must have

  • I = real stock counts and real lab certificates per lot (shared/inventory.js plus COAs).
  • K = checkout events (added to cart, order placed) from whichever checkout we connect.
  • none = can be built now, with no outside company.

02How we ranked

  • Score = impact × evidence × fit ÷ effort. Impact 1–5 (our judgement). Evidence 3 = independent study, 2 = vendor data, 1 = claim or inference. Fit 3 = works before gate zero, 1 = blocked today. Effort S = 1, M = 2, L = 3.
  • Priority 1–5 comes from the score: 5 means score 15+, 1 means under 3.
  • Compliance is a gate, not a score. Risk is rated for the compliant version of each feature. Anything still High is held at the bottom until counsel clears it.
  • Scores sort the list. They are not sales forecasts. Prices are mostly third-party trackers (Apr–Oct 2026, USD): confirm on the vendor's page before buying.

04Build first: the top 10

Cheap, mostly our own code, and backed by the strongest proof. Items 1–6 and 9 need no outside company, so they can start straight away.

  1. All-in price everywhereFixes the biggest reason people leave (40% abandon over extra costs, Baymard). The law already requires it.
    First step: show shipping estimate and total on product cards and in the cart drawer.
  2. Free-shipping barRaises basket size at almost no cost. A threshold set too high can lose buyers, so test it.
    First step: pick two thresholds and build the bar to test both.
  3. Verified service reviews (store-wide)Strongest proof on the list: 5 reviews make a purchase 270% more likely than none (Spiegel).
    First step: write the moderation rules and add "review your delivery" to the shipped email.
  4. Lot-certificate lookup and a true trust stripAnswers the 19% who don't trust a site with their card, with proof others can't fake.
    First step: get one real lab certificate for one lot and build the lookup on it.
  5. Page speed on the store path0.1 s faster on phones gave +8.4% conversions (Deloitte study for Google).
    First step: measure store and product pages on a phone; set a load budget for the 3D.
  6. Privacy-safe analyticsWe can't improve what we can't measure. Ad pixels on peptide pages repeat the GoodRx and BetterHelp fines.
    First step: install Plausible with goals for add-to-cart and checkout start.
  7. Short guest checkoutForced accounts (18%) and long forms (17%) are fixable. Aim for 7–8 fields.
    First step: lay it out with the age and province gate, ready for whichever processor says yes.
  8. Abandoned cart, with consent captureHighest-earning email flow in Klaviyo's data ($6.77 per email, vendor figure).
    First step: add the unticked consent box to every email field now; ask email platforms for written approval.
  9. "Save my cart" exit cardCatches people leaving and grows the consented list, with no outside company.
    First step: build it from the exit-and-return-hooks skill with the approved copy.
  10. Quantity breaks, same item onlyThe cleanest way to raise order value without "stacks" named for a goal.
    First step: build ×2/×3 pricing on one item; wait for counsel before applying it more widely.

05Copy check: what big stores say vs what we can say

Run with the regulated-claims check. Research-style hedging ("studied for…") counts as proof of intended use, so a disclaimer doesn't fix a bad sentence: delete it. Risk scan only, not legal advice.

#FeatureTypical big-store line (fails)Why it failsWhat we say instead

The ceiling

  • Prescription-list products (e.g. tirzepatide, HGH, tesamorelin): ads to the public may show name, price and quantity only (FDR C.01.044).
  • Unauthorized products (most of the catalogue, named in Health Canada's advisories): there is no lawful claim layer at all. The Canlab injunction (2026) is the precedent.
  • Safe to say: name, size, price in CAD, real stock, lot and certificate (once real), shipping, returns, contact, order status, and store mechanics (cart saved, points for access, gift cards).

Counsel questions (these gate the work)

  1. Can PEPTICITY lawfully sell and advertise each item in Canada, and ship to the US?
  2. Are price incentives (15% code, quantity breaks, subscribe-and-save, points, referral credit) allowed on prescription or unauthorized items?
  3. Does starting a checkout count as a CASL "inquiry", so one plain cart reminder can go out without the box ticked?
  4. Can reviews appear on product pages, or only store-wide service reviews?
  5. Which auto-renewal and cancellation rules apply to a BC seller shipping across Canada?

06Sales audit: why this could still fail

A separate reviewer, who hadn't seen the plan being written, tried to break it.

Verdict. The top 10 makes visitors more likely to buy, but nothing in it brings visitors in or takes the money. Fix those two first.

Where the money path breaks

  • No arrival. No row says where visitors come from. Google and Meta ads need LegitScript certification, which only covers approved drugs sold by licensed pharmacies or clinics, so paid ads are closed.
  • No payment. No processor is named, and #7 checkout is "design now, wire later".
  • No proof yet. #3 reviews need delivered orders. #4 certificate lookup needs real lab certificates, and the inventory file has no lot field yet.
  • Earning vs cost. Only #1 all-in price, #2 shipping bar and #5 speed earn from day one, and only once a processor exists. The rest are cost until something else exists.

Biggest miss: Grace's current store

  • Grace already runs northernpeptides.st (WooCommerce, 36 listings, the source of our catalogue). It already takes payment and has past buyers. The plan ignored it.
  • Catch: it also lists bacteriostatic water, which regulators treat as proof of intended human use. Whether that store affects PEPTICITY is a question for counsel.

Suggested order for revenue in 60 days (reviewer's view)

  1. Money received: a processor that approves the catalogue in writing, or Interac e-Transfer as a stopgap.
  2. Counsel clears a short list of products.
  3. Bring over past buyers from the current store (with consent).
  4. All-in price and guest checkout.
  5. One real lab certificate (get a lab quote).
  6. Traffic: a journal that ranks on Google for safe topics, plus showing up in peptide communities.
  7. Referrals, then reviews, then email flows, then speed and analytics polish.

What changes in the ranking

  • Add step zero: money received above the top 10. Every row that needs a processor sits below it.
  • Add getting traffic as its own track (journal, communities, referrals, clinic or pharmacy partners), scored the same way.
  • Reviews and the certificate lookup only count once orders and certificates exist.
  • Weakest top-10 items to demote if time is short: analytics, speed, quantity breaks, exit card, shipping bar.

Not checked by the reviewer: the current store's processor, order volume, email list or traffic.
