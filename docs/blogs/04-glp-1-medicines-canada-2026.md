# GLP-1 MEDICINES: WHAT CANADIANS SHOULD KNOW IN 2026

Few medicines have generated as many headlines as the GLP-1 family. In 2026 alone, Canada authorized its first generic semaglutide, Health Canada warned about counterfeit products, and a new investigational medicine reported major trial results.

If you're trying to make sense of it all, this guide lays out the facts: what these medicines are, which ones are authorized in Canada, what generics change, what "investigational" really means, and how to stay safe. It's education only. Decisions about any medicine belong with you and a licensed prescriber.

> **KEY TAKEAWAYS**
> - GLP-1 medicines are lab-made peptides based on a natural gut hormone. Several are authorized prescription drugs in Canada.
> - In 2026, Canada became the first G7 country to authorize a generic version of semaglutide.
> - Retatrutide is investigational. It is not approved anywhere, and Health Canada named it in its April 2026 peptide warning.
> - Health Canada has warned that fake or unauthorized GLP-1 products may contain the wrong amount, none of the ingredient or unknown substances.
> - The safe route is a prescription from a licensed prescriber, filled by a licensed Canadian pharmacy.

## WHAT IS GLP-1?

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a hormone your gut releases after you eat. It's part of the body's system for managing blood sugar and appetite signals. Your own GLP-1 is broken down within minutes.

GLP-1 medicines are lab-made peptides made to act on the same receptor but last much longer. Some newer medicines act on more than one hormone receptor at once, such as GLP-1 plus a related hormone called GIP.

## WHICH GLP-1 MEDICINES ARE AUTHORIZED IN CANADA?

In its January 2026 warning about counterfeit products, Health Canada listed the authorized versions of these medicines as:

- **Semaglutide:** Ozempic and Wegovy (injections) and Rybelsus (oral tablets)
- **Tirzepatide:** Mounjaro and Zepbound (injections)

Each brand is authorized for specific uses, in specific people, as described in its official product monograph. Ozempic, Rybelsus and Mounjaro are authorized for type 2 diabetes. ## GENERIC SEMAGLUTIDE ARRIVES IN CANADA

### WHAT HAPPENED
- **April 28, 2026:** Health Canada authorized Dr. Reddy's generic semaglutide injection, making Canada the first G7 country to approve a generic version.
- **May 1, 2026:** Health Canada authorized a second generic semaglutide injection, Apotex's Apo-Semaglutide.
- **Late May 2026:** Generic semaglutide began reaching Canadian pharmacies.
- **June 2026:** Health Canada approved the first generic semaglutide for chronic weight management, Apotex's Svemia.

## RETATRUTIDE: WHAT "INVESTIGATIONAL" MEANS

Retatrutide is an experimental medicine from Eli Lilly that acts on three hormone receptors (GLP-1, GIP and glucagon). It has drawn huge online attention.

Here's where it actually stands:
- In July 2026, Lilly announced that two further phase 3 trials met their main goals. Full results had not yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal.
- Lilly has said it plans to submit an application to the US Food and Drug Administration in the first quarter of 2027.
- **It is not approved in Canada, the US or anywhere else.**
- Health Canada named retatrutide in its April 2026 "Think twice before injecting peptides bought online" advisory.

"Investigational" means the medicine is still being studied. Its benefits and risks haven't yet been reviewed by a regulator, and its long-term safety profile isn't known. Anything sold online as "retatrutide" today hasn't been through that review, and you have no way of knowing what's in it.

## THE COUNTERFEIT PROBLEM

In January 2026, Health Canada warned Canadians not to buy or use fake or unauthorized versions of GLP-1 medicines, saying they were being sold in stores and online. According to the warning, these products:

- may contain **too much, too little or none** of the active ingredient;
- may contain **unlisted, dangerous or unknown** substances;
- are **illegal to sell**.

Health Canada advised getting these medicines **only with a prescription, from a licensed pharmacy**, and reporting suspected counterfeits and side effects.

### WHAT ABOUT COMPOUNDED VERSIONS?
Compounding is when a pharmacy prepares a medicine for an individual patient. In June 2025, the Alberta College of Pharmacy shared Health Canada's position that compounding injectable GLP-1 medicines when there is no shortage "is not permitted". With brand and generic products now available in Canada, that context matters. In the US, the FDA ended its shortage-era flexibility for tirzepatide and semaglutide compounding in 2024 and 2025 and has sent many warning letters to sellers since.

## SAFETY: WHAT TO KNOW BEFORE ANY CONVERSATION

Even authorized GLP-1 medicines aren't right for everyone. Their product monographs list side effects and warnings, and digestive side effects are commonly reported. Some groups, such as people who are pregnant or have certain personal or family medical histories, may need to avoid them or be monitored closely. ## HOW TO STAY SAFE
- **Get a prescription** from a licensed prescriber you trust.
- **Use a licensed Canadian pharmacy.** Your provincial pharmacy regulator can confirm a pharmacy is licensed.
- **Check the DIN** on the package in Health Canada's Drug Product Database.
- **Avoid** "research use only" products, social-media sellers and anything sold without a prescription.
- **Report** suspected counterfeits and side effects to Health Canada. In an emergency, call 911.

## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

### IS GENERIC OZEMPIC AVAILABLE IN CANADA?
Yes. Health Canada authorized generic semaglutide injections from Dr. Reddy's (April 28, 2026) and Apotex (May 1, 2026), and generic product began reaching pharmacies in late May 2026. A prescription is required.

### IS RETATRUTIDE APPROVED IN CANADA?
No. Retatrutide is investigational and isn't approved anywhere. Health Canada named it in its April 2026 warning about unauthorized peptides sold online.

### WHICH GLP-1 MEDICINES ARE AUTHORIZED IN CANADA?
Health Canada lists semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) as authorized, plus authorized generic semaglutide products. Each is authorized for specific uses described in its product monograph.

### HOW CAN I TELL IF MY GLP-1 MEDICINE IS REAL?
Get it with a prescription from a licensed pharmacy, check the DIN in Health Canada's Drug Product Database and be wary of unusually low prices, missing labels or sellers on social media.

### IS COMPOUNDED SEMAGLUTIDE LEGAL IN CANADA?
Health Canada's position, as shared by the Alberta College of Pharmacy in June 2025, is that compounding injectable GLP-1 medicines without a shortage is not permitted. Ask your pharmacist about your situation.
