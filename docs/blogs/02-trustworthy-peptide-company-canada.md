# HOW TO SPOT A TRUSTWORTHY PEPTIDE COMPANY IN CANADA IN 2026

Peptides are having a moment, and so are the websites selling them. Some look polished. Some offer big discounts. Many carry a small line saying "for research use only".

In 2026, Health Canada made it clear that a polished website isn't proof of anything. This guide explains what the regulator has actually said and done this year, how to read a certificate of analysis, and the red flags worth walking away from.

> **KEY TAKEAWAYS**
> - Health Canada says injectable peptides are regulated as prescription drugs and must be authorized before they can be sold in Canada.
> - In 2026, Health Canada issued a peptide advisory (April), a counterfeit GLP-1 warning (January) and won a permanent court injunction against an online peptide seller (June).
> - A "research use only" label does not make selling to people legal, in Canada or the US.
> - A certificate of analysis (COA) is useful, but it is not regulatory approval and can be faked. Check that the lot number matches and that the lab is real.
> - Trust comes from accountability: a real company, honest labels, no medical promises and no instructions from people who aren't your health professional.

## WHAT CHANGED IN 2026

### THE WARNINGS

In January 2026, Health Canada warned Canadians not to buy or use fake or unauthorized GLP-1 products, such as unauthorized semaglutide, saying they were being sold in stores and online. It said such products may contain too much, too little or none of the active ingredient, or unlisted and dangerous substances, and that Canadians should get these medicines only with a prescription, from a licensed pharmacy.

In April 2026, Health Canada published "Think twice before injecting peptides bought online". It named products including BPC-157, CJC-1295, Epitalon, GHK-Cu, ipamorelin, KPV, Melanotan I and II, MOTS-c, NAD+, SS-31, TB-500 and retatrutide, and warned that unauthorized products can seriously harm you.

### THE SEIZURES

Health Canada has publicly announced seizures of unauthorized injectable peptides, including roughly 50 products from a seller called Canada Peptide in 2025 and products from a Calgary wellness centre.

### THE COURT ORDER

The biggest shift was legal. Health Canada first warned Canadians about products from Canlab Research, a Québec online retailer, in December 2023. It then obtained a provisional injunction in 2024 and an interlocutory injunction in 2025. On June 11, 2026, the Superior Court of Québec granted a **permanent injunction** barring Canlab Research and its representatives from manufacturing, testing, distributing or selling unauthorized injectable peptides in Canada. Health Canada announced the order on July 29, 2026, and said it will monitor compliance, which could include a referral for prosecution or a contempt application.

Law firms reading the case put it simply: enforcement has moved from warnings to the courts.

### THE US PICTURE

South of the border, the US Food and Drug Administration sent warning letters on August 24, 2026 to several online peptide sellers whose products were marketed as GLP-1-type compounds. Legal commentary on those letters noted that the FDA looked at the whole presentation, including dosing information and items sold alongside, and did not accept "research use only" labels as a defence.

## "RESEARCH USE ONLY": WHAT IT REALLY MEANS

"Research use only" is a real category. Laboratories, universities and biotech companies buy research chemicals every day. But when a consumer website uses that label while also offering dosing charts, "beginner guides", calculators or mixing supplies, regulators in both countries read the whole picture, not the label.

Health Canada's April 2026 advisory makes the same point from the consumer's side: a disclaimer doesn't change what is in the vial, and it doesn't make an unauthorized product safe or legal to sell to you.

## A CHECKLIST FOR JUDGING ANY PEPTIDE COMPANY

### 1. CHECK THE LEGAL STATUS FIRST
- **Prescription drugs** authorized in Canada carry an eight-digit Drug Identification Number (DIN). You can search it in Health Canada's Drug Product Database.
- **Natural health products** carry an eight-digit Natural Product Number (NPN) and can be checked in Health Canada's Licensed Natural Health Products Database. [VERIFY database name before publishing]
- **Cosmetics** don't get a number, and they can't legally make drug claims.
- If an injectable product has no DIN, ask why.

### 2. READ THE LABEL LIKE A SKEPTIC
An honest label says what the product is, how much is in it, a lot (batch) number, an expiry date, storage conditions and who made it, with a real company name and address. Vague, missing or "for research only" labels on a product clearly marketed to the public are a warning sign.

### 3. KNOW WHAT A CERTIFICATE OF ANALYSIS CAN AND CAN'T TELL YOU
A certificate of analysis (COA) is a lab report on one specific batch. A useful COA:
- shows **the same lot number** that's printed on your product;
- names **the testing lab**, ideally an independent one you can contact to confirm the report;
- gives **the test date** and **the methods** (for example, HPLC for purity and mass spectrometry for identity);
- reports more than purity where relevant, such as checks for contamination.

What a COA *can't* do: it isn't regulatory approval, it says nothing about whether a product is right for you, and it can be copied, edited or reused across batches. A COA with no lot number is general marketing, not proof about the vial in your hand.

### 4. WATCH WHAT THEY TELL YOU TO DO
A seller is not your health professional. Websites that hand out dosing schedules, "cycles", "stacks" or mixing instructions are giving medical instructions without knowing anything about you. That is a red flag on its own, and it was a key factor in the US enforcement letters.

### 5. LISTEN FOR PROMISES
Canada's Competition Act requires that performance claims be backed by adequate and proper testing done *before* the claim is made. Big promises about results for products with only animal studies behind them can't meet that bar.

### 6. LOOK AT THE PRICE AND THE PRESSURE
Canada's Competition Bureau has acted against fake countdown timers and false scarcity, and courts have penalized "drip pricing", where mandatory fees appear late. A trustworthy company shows the full price up front and doesn't rush you.

### 7. ASK WHO IS ACCOUNTABLE
Can you find a registered company name, a physical address, a Canadian contact and a clear privacy policy? Will they answer a direct question in writing? Accountability is the quiet foundation of trust.

## RED FLAGS AT A GLANCE
- "Research use only" next to dosing charts, calculators or injection supplies
- Big discounts, "drop alerts" or countdown timers on injectable products
- Before-and-after photos or testimonials about results
- COAs without lot numbers, or the same COA for every batch
- No company name, address or way to reach a real person
- "Same as" or "just like" a prescription brand
- Claims that a product is "Health Canada approved" with no DIN you can check

## IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
If you've had a side effect from a health product, seek medical care and report it to Health Canada through its side-effect reporting system (MedEffect Canada). You can also report a suspected unauthorized product to Health Canada. In an emergency, call 911.

## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

### IS IT LEGAL TO BUY PEPTIDES ONLINE IN CANADA?
Health Canada says injectable peptides are regulated as prescription drugs and must be authorized before they can be sold, and it has called the sale of unauthorized injectable peptides illegal. Authorized peptide medicines, such as insulin and GLP-1 medicines, are available with a prescription through licensed pharmacies.

### WHAT HAPPENED WITH CANLAB RESEARCH?
On June 11, 2026, the Superior Court of Québec granted Health Canada a permanent injunction barring Canlab Research and its representatives from manufacturing, testing, distributing or selling unauthorized injectable peptides in Canada. Health Canada announced it on July 29, 2026.

### DOES A CERTIFICATE OF ANALYSIS MEAN A PEPTIDE IS SAFE?
No. A COA is a lab report on one batch. It can be useful if the lot number matches and the lab is real, but it isn't regulatory approval and doesn't show a product is safe or suitable for you.

### WHAT DOES "RESEARCH USE ONLY" MEAN ON A PEPTIDE?
It means the product isn't meant for people. Regulators in Canada and the US have said the label doesn't make it legal to sell a product to consumers for human use.

### HOW DO I REPORT AN UNAUTHORIZED HEALTH PRODUCT IN CANADA?
You can report side effects and suspected unauthorized health products to Health Canada online. If you've been harmed, get medical care first.
